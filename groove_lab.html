GROOVE (Generated Real-time Output Operations on Voltage-controlled Equipment) was built at Bell Labs around 1970 by Max Mathews and F. Richard Moore. In it, the computer did not make the sound. It sampled a performer's knobs and joystick about 100 times a second, stored them on disk as functions of time, and played them back as control voltages into an analog synthesizer. A performer could record one function at a time, redraw it, and conduct the playback by hand.

Touching a knob or the joystick takes over that channel: a channel in Play switches to Live, so you hear your hand instead of the stored score. Click Play on the channel to hand control back.
Try this: press Run to hear the stored score. Set Pitch to Rec and drag around the joystick to record a new melody over it; after one pass it switches back to Play. Then record Loudness, or draw straight onto a graph. Turn Bend on to push a stored function up or down live, and use the conductor slider to speed the whole score up or slow it down.
