Listening to a Program

A loudspeaker wired to an early computer. Build music out of loops, watch the machine step through them, and hear what the speaker hears.

Every note is a loop. One trip round the loop pulses the speaker once, so pitch = machine speed ÷ loop length: a 61-instruction loop on a 16,000 instr/s machine gives ≈ 262 Hz (middle C), and a 122-instruction loop gives the C an octave below. A Repeat block becomes a counter and a backward branch (DEC + JNZ); a Forever block is a plain backward JMP; rests are SLEEPs, and the silences between notes are what make rhythm. Change the machine speed and every loop keeps its length but its pitch moves, exactly as it would on real hardware. Speeds are approximate; verify them before citing.
